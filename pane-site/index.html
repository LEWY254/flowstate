Running one Windows program shouldn't mean running all of Windows. Pane skips the second desktop and gives you just the app, behaving like it belongs on your Mac.

Why Pane

It feels like it came from the Mac.

It lives in Finder

Every Windows app becomes its own .app. Find it in Applications, open it from Spotlight or Launchpad, and keep it in the Dock.

Saves where you expect

Hit Save and the file lands in your real Desktop, Documents or Downloads. It isn't buried in a hidden drive you have to dig through.

No virtual machine

Pane doesn't boot a copy of Windows. It translates the app's requests for your Mac as it runs, so it starts fast and doesn't need a Windows licence.

Games, too

DirectX 9 through 12 is translated to Metal, including Apple's D3DMetal for DirectX 11 and 12 on Apple Silicon.

One app, one bundle

Each app keeps its own settings, fonts and add-ons in its own bundle. Delete the app and it all goes with it.

Stays on your Mac

Your apps and files never leave your machine. There's no cloud streaming and no account just to open a program.

How it works

Three steps, then it's just an app.

  1. 1

    Drop in the installer

    Drag a Windows .exe or .msi onto Pane, or pick the app's folder.

  2. 2

    Pane builds a Mac app

    It installs the program, chooses the right graphics settings and wraps it all in a single .app with its own icon.

  3. 3

    Open it like any other app

    Launch it from Finder, Spotlight or the Dock. Your files save to your Mac, ready for AirDrop, iCloud or Time Machine.

FAQ

Good questions.

Will every Windows app work?

Not every one, and we won't pretend otherwise. Most everyday programs and many games run well. Apps with kernel-level anti-cheat or special hardware drivers usually don't.

Do I need a copy of Windows?

No. Pane is built on Wine, which runs Windows programs directly on macOS without Windows itself. You only need the app you want to run.

Which Macs are supported?

macOS 14.6 Sonoma or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel. Apple Silicon Macs also need Rosetta 2, which macOS offers to install the first time it's needed.

Where do my files go?

The app's Desktop, Documents and Downloads are your Mac's real folders. Anything you save shows up in Finder straight away.

My antivirus flagged a file. Is it safe?

Windows programs compiled for Wine sometimes trip antivirus heuristics. That's a known false positive across Wine-based tools. Only download Pane from this site.
